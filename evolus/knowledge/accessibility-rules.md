# Audio description in lessons: what the rules ask for

Audio description is a voice that says what is on screen, in the pauses of a video, for people who cannot see it. These are the rules schools most often ask about.

## WCAG 2.1 (W3C)

- **1.2.3 Audio Description or Media Alternative (Prerecorded), level A.** Recorded video needs audio description, or a full text alternative.
- **1.2.5 Audio Description (Prerecorded), level AA.** Recorded video needs audio description. Level AA is what most laws point to.
- **1.2.7 Extended Audio Description (Prerecorded), level AAA.** When the pauses in a video are too short to describe what matters, the video is paused while the description plays. Sottovoce does this for every lesson: on two NASA lessons, 24 of 32 descriptions did not fit in the teacher's pauses.

Source: W3C, Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.1, https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG21/

## United States: ADA Title II

- The 2024 rule requires state and local governments, including public schools, colleges and universities, to meet WCAG 2.1 AA for web content and mobile apps, course video included. ADA.gov says there is no exception for educational course content.
- An interim final rule of 20 April 2026 moved the compliance dates to 26 April 2027 (public entities serving 50,000 people or more) and 26 April 2028 (smaller entities and special districts).

Source: ADA.gov, Title II web and mobile accessibility rule and its fact sheet.

## Italy and the EU

- **Legge Stanca** (Legge 9 gennaio 2004, n. 4) requires public administrations, schools and universities included, to make their digital content accessible. AgID's accessibility guidelines apply the European standard EN 301 549, which includes WCAG 2.1 AA.
- **Students with disabilities** have a support teacher (insegnante di sostegno) and an individual education plan (PEI) under Legge 104/1992. Accessible lesson material is part of that plan.
- The **European Accessibility Act** covers services such as electronic programme guides and passing on audio description. It does not by itself require schools to produce audio description.

Sources: AgID, Linee guida sull'accessibilità degli strumenti informatici; Legge 4/2004; Legge 104/1992; Directive (EU) 2019/882.

## How common described lessons are

In a national survey by the Described and Captioned Media Program, educators of blind students said fewer than 5% of the educational videos they use were described.
