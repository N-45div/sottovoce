    How it works

    From a film to a described film, with a person in charge.

    1. 01

      Finds the pauses

      It transcribes the dialogue with word timings, so a description never talks over anyone.

    2. 02

      Looks at the right frames

      It finds every shot change and picks a keyframe from each shot inside a pause. Black frames are skipped.

    3. 03

      Writes, then checks

      Gemma 4 writes the line. Qwen3.8, a different model family, checks every claim against the frames and sends wrong ones back.

    4. 04

      Voices it to fit

      Google's Chirp 3 HD voice reads the line. Every take is measured; a line that cannot fit its pause goes to a person.

    5. 05

      Mixes like a broadcaster

      The programme dips under each line. You get an MP4, a separate narration track and a WebVTT descriptions file.

    6. 06

      A describer signs off

      Only verified or approved lines are aired. Edit any line and it is checked, re-voiced and re-mixed in about half a minute.

    The checker

    A second model reads every line against the picture.

    The checker splits each line into claims about people, objects, places, colours and on-screen text, and checks each one against the keyframes. We tested it by planting a false detail in every line it had already approved.

    Here, "a man" became "a child". The checker caught it.

    Who it is for

    Teams who have to describe more than they can afford to.

    Broadcasters and streamers

    RAI's contract ramps audio description on its thematic channels to 60% by 2029, and AGCOM accessibility plans apply from 2026. Sottovoce drafts and checks; your describers approve.

    Universities and public bodies

    WCAG 2.1 AA requires audio description for recorded video. US public bodies must comply from April 2027. Course and council video is the backlog nobody can afford to describe by hand.

    Museums and heritage sites

    Describe exhibition films once and serve Italian, English and extended versions to every visitor.

    Post-production houses

    Deliver a separate narration track and a WebVTT file alongside the mix, and keep the open models on your own AMD GPUs if the footage cannot leave the building.

    For developers

    Another agent can ask for a line.

    The describe-and-check loop is a Google ADK agent, also served over the A2A protocol. Send keyframes and get back a line with every claim's verdict. The agent card is at /a2a/.well-known/agent-card.json.

    Questions

    Before you try it.

    How accurate is it?

    On our test film the checker caught 28 of 32 planted errors and flagged none of 32 correct lines. It still misses swaps that leave the picture plausible, such as a beach for the deck of a ship. That is why nothing reaches the audience unless the checker verified it or a person approved it.

    What runs on AMD?

    Every vision call. Gemma 4 writes and Qwen3.8 checks, both open models on AMD Instinct GPUs through AMD Token Factory. If one model is down, a backup on the same platform takes over.

    Which languages and formats?

    Italian, English for visitors, and extended Italian, where the picture holds while a longer line is spoken. You get an MP4 with the description mixed in, a separate narration WAV and a WebVTT descriptions track.

    Can our footage stay private?

    The hosted version keeps uploads in a private bucket in the EU (Milan) and sends only keyframes and dialogue snippets to AMD Token Factory for the vision models. The models are open, so a broadcaster can run them on its own AMD GPUs and keep everything in the building.

    Does it describe people's appearance?

    It describes what people do and wear when it matters, and names them only once the dialogue does. It never describes skin colour or ethnicity.
