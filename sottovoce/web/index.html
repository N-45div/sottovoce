    The problem

    "Look at this" leaves a blind child out.

    Lessons are full of things only the eyes get: the formula on the board, the arrow on a diagram, the colour change in an experiment. Describing a video takes a trained describer, so almost none are described, and support teachers fill the gap as best they can.

    How it works

    A lesson that waits for the child.

    On two NASA lessons, 24 of 32 descriptions were longer than the teacher's pause. Ordinary audio description would drop them or rush them. Sottovoce made the lesson wait: 100 seconds over 8.5 minutes of lesson.

    1. 01

      Listens to the teacher

      It transcribes the lesson and finds every moment the teacher points at the screen: "look at this", "you can see", "guardate", "ecco".

    2. 02

      Pauses when it matters

      Right after the teacher points, in the natural pauses, and whenever the picture keeps changing with nothing described.

    3. 03

      Writes for a child

      A vision model reads the board, numbers and formulas exactly, explains a diagram by how it is built, and uses short sentences and simple words.

    4. 04

      Checks every claim

      Qwen3.8, a different model family, checks each claim against the picture. A wrong number would teach a child something false, so it goes back.

    5. 05

      Speaks while the lesson waits

      A natural voice reads the description and the video holds until it ends. Nothing the teacher says is talked over.

    6. 06

      The teacher approves

      Only verified or approved lines reach the child. Edit any line and it is checked, re-voiced and re-mixed in about half a minute.

        Who it is for

        The people who get a lesson to a blind child.

        Support teachers

        Prepare a described lesson in minutes instead of improvising at the desk. Read every line, approve the few the checker could not confirm, and fix any in one click.

        Schools and universities

        WCAG 2.1 AA asks for audio description on recorded video. In the US the deadline is April 2027; in Italy the Legge Stanca applies to public schools and universities.

        Families

        Describe the videos a child is set for homework, so they can follow along without someone beside them.

        Publishers and learning platforms

        Ship a described version of every video in a catalogue, with a WebVTT descriptions track that works in any player.

        For developers

        A learning platform can ask for a line.

        The describe-and-check loop is a Google ADK agent, also served over the A2A protocol. Send keyframes and get back a line with every claim's verdict. The agent card is at /a2a/.well-known/agent-card.json.

        Questions

        Before you try it.

        How accurate is it?

        Every line is checked against the picture by a second model, and we measure that checker by planting false details. Nothing reaches a child unless the checker verified it or a teacher approved it.

        What if the teacher never stops talking?

        The lesson waits. Sottovoce holds the picture after the sentence, speaks the description, then lets the teacher carry on. It also steps in when the picture keeps changing and nothing has been described for a while.

        Which languages and ages?

        Italian and English, written for children of about 8 to 13. You get the described video, a separate narration track and a WebVTT descriptions file.

        What runs on AMD?

        Every vision call. Gemma 4 writes and Qwen3.8 checks, both open models on AMD Instinct GPUs through AMD Token Factory. When Gemma 4 was taken off Token Factory for a day (Oct 16), the DeepSeek backups on the same platform wrote every line, and each line says which model wrote it.

        Can our lessons stay private?

        The hosted version keeps uploads in a private bucket in the EU (Milan) and sends only keyframes and transcript snippets to AMD Token Factory. The models are open, so a school network or publisher can run them on its own AMD GPUs.

        Does it describe people's appearance?

        It describes what people do and hold when it matters for the lesson. It never describes skin colour or ethnicity.

        Describe your first lesson.

        Upload up to five minutes of video. Watch every description get written and checked, then listen as a child would.
